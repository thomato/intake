# Daily Dozen and 21 Tweaks: source content and reuse rights

Research for the wayfinder ticket "Daily Dozen and 21 Tweaks: the source content and reuse rights". Sources were read on 2026-11-02.

**This is not legal advice.** Register entries and licence terms below are reported as the source states them. Anything marked *Inference* is my reading and needs a lawyer before a paid release.

## Answer

- **Content:** the Daily Dozen is 12 daily checklist items, and the 21 Tweaks are 21 weight-loss habits. Official amounts for both are in the tables below.
- **Evidence:** the sources present the Dozen as Greger's own evidence-informed checklist, not a tested protocol. The only direct test I found is an uncontrolled eight-person master's thesis, which Greger himself calls weak evidence.
- **Name:** `DR. GREGER'S DAILY DOZEN` is a live US registration held by Greger personally, and it covers this exact kind of app. The site's terms also require written approval to use his name.
- **Text and code:** site materials are CC BY-NC, so that licence gives no commercial permission. The app code is GPLv3, which doesn't suit a closed paid app.
- **Way forward (*Inference*):** personal use looks low risk. For a paid release, either get written permission or ship independently worded food-group guidance under a different name, with no Greger branding and no copied text, images or code. Keep the labels and guidance in swappable data so this stays open.

The page also says:

- **B12 supplement:** at least 2,000 mcg of cyanocobalamin once a week, or at least 50 mcg a day. This sits outside the 12 items. The app's data model also has vitamin D and omega-3 entries, but the page says the app now reflects only required supplements.
- **Calories:** an average of about 1,200, with a range of about 800 to 1,800 depending on food choices.
- **How to use it:** the FAQ calls it an aspirational guide that is not a meal plan and not prescriptive. It sends children, and people who are pregnant or breastfeeding, to a professional for individual needs.

The page offers a Dutch infographic, which may help with the Netherlands focus. Its download restrictions are covered under reuse.

## The 21 Tweaks

The App Store listing describes these as [weight-loss accelerators based on *How Not to Diet*](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dr-gregers-daily-dozen/id1060700802). I found no public NutritionFacts.org page that lists them; the [weight-loss topic page](https://nutritionfacts.org/topics/weight-loss/) only refers to them. The list below is therefore paraphrased from the [app's English data file](https://github.com/nutritionfactsorg/daily-dozen-ios/blob/master/DailyDozen/DailyDozen/Texts/LocalStrings/en.lproj/TweakDetailData.json). Daily check-off counts are from the [iOS code](https://github.com/nutritionfactsorg/daily-dozen-ios/blob/master/DailyDozen/DailyDozen/Database/Data/DataCountAttributes.swift). I did not compare this list against the book itself.

| Each meal | "Negative calorie" preload | Start with an apple, or a soup or salad under 100 kcal a cup | 3 |

| Daily | Nutritional yeast | 2 tsp | 1 |
| Daily | Cumin | ½ tsp with lunch and with dinner | 2 |
| Daily | Green tea | 3 cups, between meals | 3 |
| Daily | Hydration | Urine stays pale, or 9 cups (women) or 13 cups (men) of unsweetened drinks if eating riboflavin-fortified foods | 1 |
| Daily | "Deflour" | Whole-grain servings eaten as intact grains | 1 |
| Daily | Front-load calories | Largest meal at breakfast or lunch | 1 |

| Daily | Weigh twice | On waking and just before bed | 2 |

Some source text carries safety warnings that any reuse needs to consider:

- **Trendelenburg:** the source warns of dizziness and fall risk on getting up. It lists people who should not try it, including those with heart, lung, reflux, brain or eye conditions or a family history of glaucoma, and says to ask a physician first.
- **Hydration:** the source says people with heart or kidney issues should talk to a physician before increasing fluids.
- **Diabetes:** exercise timing advice differs for diabetics and prediabetics.

## Evidence and reasoning, as the sources state them

**Daily Dozen**

- The page describes it as a checklist of some of the healthiest foods, ["based on the best available balance of evidence"](https://nutritionfacts.org/daily-dozen/).
- The core argument is that [plant foods are not interchangeable](https://nutritionfacts.org/2021/07/29/dr-gregers-daily-dozen-healthiest-of-healthy-foods/), because some contain compounds rarely found elsewhere. The examples given are sulforaphane in cruciferous vegetables, lignans in flax and ergothioneine in mushrooms.
- Greger says it began as his personal fridge checklist. The [checklist video](https://nutritionfacts.org/video/dr-gregers-daily-dozen-checklist/) gives the amounts but not how they were derived. The repos say he [explains his rationale in *How Not to Die*](https://github.com/nutritionfactsorg/daily-dozen-ios#about), which I did not read.

**21 Tweaks**

- The app text gives a basis for individual tweaks. It cites a systematic review and meta-analysis for black cumin, and randomized trials for garlic, ginger and nutritional yeast.
- It is candid on weighing: it says evidence is insufficient to support a specific frequency, and its twice-daily advice rests on one study.

**Direct test of the combined approach**

- A [2021 master's thesis](https://lsmu.lt/cris/entities/etd/01f5b583-8154-4f0f-b717-3f4de929227f) at the Lithuanian University of Health Sciences studied eight overweight or obese participants, with a four-week intervention.
- [Greger's video on it](https://nutritionfacts.org/video/daily-dozen-diet-put-to-the-test-for-weight-loss/) reports about 15 lb (6.80 kg) average loss and calls a small, uncontrolled study "super-weak sauce evidence".
- The record I read through a summarising tool gave about 6.8% loss rather than 6.80 kg. I did not reconcile the two figures.

**Independent critique**

I found no independent critique or controlled trial of the Dozen or the Tweaks as such. The nearest reviews cover the books:

- [Red Pen Reviews on *How Not to Diet*](https://www.redpenreviews.org/reviews/how-not-to-diet/) (2024) scores scientific accuracy 2/4, reference accuracy 3.2/4 and healthfulness 3/4. Of its three sampled claims, the anti-inflammatory and "obesogen" claims were poorly supported and the fibre claim well supported. It does not assess the Tweaks list.
- [Denise Minger's Healthline review of *How Not to Die*](https://www.healthline.com/nutrition/how-not-to-die-review) (2017) alleges cherry-picking and misrepresented studies. It does not discuss the Dozen, and it is a less rigorous source than a formal review.

*Inference:* the app should present these as Greger's recommendations, not as established targets or medical advice. The source does the same: its [terms](https://nutritionfacts.org/terms-of-service/) say the site gives no medical advice.

## Reuse rights

### What the sources say

**Trademarks.** From the [USPTO status record](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/statusview/sn90273898), read on 2026-11-02, unless noted:

| Mark | Number | Status | Owner | Covers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `DR. GREGER'S DAILY DOZEN` | Reg. 6662527, Serial 90273898 | Live, Principal Register, registered 2022-03-08 | Michael Greger, individual | Class 9: downloadable mobile, handheld and tablet software for managing healthy eating, dietary and nutritional guidance and healthy weight |
| `HOW NOT TO DIE` | Reg. 6909359, Serial 90273905 | Live per [USPTO search](https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/); its status page returned 403 | Michael Greger, individual | Class 16: book series |
| `THE DAILY DOZEN: HABITS OF HIGHLY SUCCESSFUL FUNDRAISERS` | Reg. 5607116 | Live per USPTO search | Unrelated company | Class 41: online fundraising instruction |

- The first registration is in standard characters, so it is not tied to one font or style. It records an acquired-distinctiveness claim in part, "as to DAILY DOZEN".
- Other "Daily Dozen" records found are dead: a 2007 entertainment application and a golf-news registration held by the PGA Tour.
- USPTO searches for owner "NutritionFacts.org" and for marks containing "21 tweaks" or "twenty-one tweaks" returned nothing. An exact-phrase search doesn't rule out variants.
- The [USPTO requires a first maintenance filing](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/maintain/keeping-your-registration-alive) between years five and six. *Inference:* for this registration, that window runs from March 2027 to March 2028.

**Site terms.** The [Terms of Service](https://nutritionfacts.org/terms-of-service/) (page metadata shows a change on 2026-08-07) and [Copyright Guidelines](https://nutritionfacts.org/copyright/) say:

- Original materials, meaning videos, blogs, podcasts and social posts, are under CC BY-NC 4.0, with credit required and alterations noted. A takedown can be requested if an alteration distorts the information.
- Non-commercial means not primarily intended for commercial advantage or monetary compensation.
- The logo, and Dr. Greger's photo and name, fall outside that licence. Using them needs written approval from a NutritionFacts.org staff member.
- Content can't be used to promote a product without staff approval.
- Original images and health kit resources may not be altered.
- Certain words, phrases, names, designs and logos are marks of NutritionFacts.org or others, and displaying them grants no licence.

**App repos.**

- The [iOS](https://github.com/nutritionfactsorg/daily-dozen-ios) and [Android](https://github.com/nutritionfactsorg/daily-dozen-android) READMEs state GPLv3, and each has a GPLv3 `LICENSE.md`. The [open-source page](https://nutritionfacts.org/open-source/) invites contributions but names no licence.
- Source headers can differ. For example, [`TweakTextsProvider.swift`](https://github.com/nutritionfactsorg/daily-dozen-ios/blob/master/DailyDozen/DailyDozen/Texts/TweakTextsProvider.swift) says "All rights reserved".
- The [localization repo](https://github.com/nutritionfactsorg/daily-dozen-localization) reports no licence through the GitHub API.
- The [app's privacy page](https://nutritionfacts.org/daily-dozen/privacy/), which the App Store listing links to, contains no licence terms for the content.
- A GitHub search of the organisation's issues for licence, commercial and trademark terms returned only one hit, a translation issue with a title unrelated to reuse, which I did not open. I found no published permission for third-party apps.

**General law.**

- US copyright [does not extend to any idea, procedure, system or method](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/102), and [a "mere listing of ingredients or contents" isn't protected](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/202.1).
- [*Feist*](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/499/340) holds that facts aren't copyrightable, and that a compilation's copyright is "thin", limited to its selection and arrangement.
- [Article 14 of the EU trade mark regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2017/1001/oj/eng) limits a mark's effects for descriptive terms and for referring to the owner's goods, subject to honest practices. EUR-Lex wouldn't load for me, so this rests on search excerpts.

### *Inference:* what that means for this app

Not legal advice.

| Layer | Personal use | Public or paid release |
|---|---|---|
| Name "Dr. Greger's Daily Dozen" | Low practical risk if private | **Avoid without permission.** It is registered for this kind of app, and his name needs written approval |
| Name "Daily Dozen" alone | Low practical risk if private | **Avoid as a product or feature name.** The phrase is singled out in the registration record, and the shared context increases confusion risk even if the phrase is weak alone |
| "21 Tweaks" | Low practical risk if private | No registration found, but it is closely tied to Greger's book. **Prefer a neutral label** |
| Food groups, counts and amounts | Fine | Facts and systems are the strongest ground. Copying the exact selection and arrangement of 12 items is the remaining "thin" copyright question, particularly for the Dozen as a whole |
| Explanatory text, video and blog text | CC BY-NC fits | **Don't copy.** Write independently. Some app text reads like the book, for example first-person advice and references to book sections |
| Infographics and images | Private viewing only | **Don't use.** They can't be altered, and a commercial licence isn't offered |
| App code | GPLv3 is workable if kept open | **Don't copy into a closed app.** GPLv3 requires derivative releases to stay under GPLv3, and the [FSF considers App Store terms incompatible with the GPL](https://www.fsf.org/blogs/licensing/more-about-the-app-store-gpl-enforcement). Writing the app independently avoids this |

**A factual mention is a separate risk.** A line like "not affiliated with Dr. Greger" may be allowed as referential use, but the terms ask for written approval to use his name, and a paid app that uses it to attract customers is the risky case. Don't rely on it without advice.

**The two safe routes for a paid release:**

1. **Get written permission.** Ask NutritionFacts.org for the content and name use, and make sure it also binds Greger, because the register shows him personally as owner while the terms point to NutritionFacts.org staff. Its [nonprofit status](https://nutritionfacts.org/) and open-source app could go either way on a commercial request.
2. **Ship a neutral version.** Use original wording and a neutral name, and let users edit items and targets. Consider whether a differently selected and grouped checklist is the safer starting point than an exact 12-item copy.

**For the v1 spec:**

- Keep checklist items, labels, amounts and guidance in data, not hard-coded.
- Keep the Greger names out of the app ID, store listing, marketing and repo-level product name.
- Treat Greger-specific text as replaceable.

## Gaps

- **EU and Benelux registers not checked.** EUIPO, TMview, BOIP and WIPO's Brand Database didn't return usable results to scripted requests. [Trademarkia](https://www.trademarkia.com/search/trademarks?query=daily%20dozen&country=eu) showed zero results for EU, UK, Germany and the Netherlands, but that is a weak secondary signal, so absence is not established. Search [TMview](https://www.tmdn.org/tmview/) and [BOIP](https://www.boip.int/en/trademarks-register) by hand before release.
- **The books weren't read.** Rationale and the full Tweaks wording in the books are known only through NutritionFacts.org and app material.
- **Android app data not compared.** Counts come from the iOS code. The two apps may differ.
- **Page reading.** Some pages, including the *Feist* opinion, the FSF post and the two critiques, were read through a summarising tool. I checked the terms, Dozen page, USPTO record and app data files directly.

## Sources not relied on

Many search results were low-trust "printable" pages or summaries, and I didn't use them. The official repos are maintained by NutritionFacts.org, and their copy of the app's text was the best public source for the Tweaks.
